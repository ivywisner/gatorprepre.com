Short Answer
Plan on $100–$500 for the course, and three to eight weeks to get through it. Florida requires a single 63-hour pre-license course — a fraction of Texas's 180 hours or California's 135 — and every FREC-approved school teaches the same state-set curriculum. Price buys polish and support, not different material. The trap isn't the money or the hours: it's the course final exam's retake rules.
The Price

Same Curriculum, Very Different Price Tags

Florida requires one course — the 63-hour FREC-approved sales associate pre-license course — and that's the entire education requirement. The content is set by FREC, so a $100 package and a $500 package teach to the same state-mandated curriculum. The $400 gap buys production quality, support, and study-tool upsells — worth paying for if they help you actually finish, but never a different certificate.

Most online students land in the low end of the range. And one group skips this line item entirely: active licensees from ten other states, via Florida's mutual recognition program.

The Real Timeline

The Clock Nobody Advertises

The 63-hour course is the long pole of the whole Florida timeline — most people take three to eight weeks to get through it around a job and a life. But the clock that actually bites is the one at the end: the course final exam. You get two attempts. Fail the first, and Florida law makes you wait 30 days before the second. Fail that one too, and you repeat the course. (The state exam, by contrast, only makes you wait 24 hours between attempts — Florida's rules are strictest at the gate you'd least expect.) A casual swing at the course final can quietly add a month — or a whole re-enrollment — to your timeline.

The fix is unglamorous: treat the course final like the real thing. Drill practice questions alongside the coursework so the final is a checkpoint, not a gamble — and the same preparation carries you straight into the state exam.

💡

The Florida timeline trick: overlap the paperwork. Your DBPR application ($62.75) and Livescan fingerprints don't have to wait for your course certificate — file while you're still mid-course and the state's 10–30 day review runs in parallel instead of in series. The full timeline guide shows how the overlap gets people from zero to licensed in 6–14 weeks.

Online vs. Classroom

The Education Is Online. The Exam Isn't.

The 63-hour course, its final exam, the DBPR application, and exam scheduling can all be done from your couch. The state exam is the exception: since March 2024, Florida no longer offers at-home online proctoring — every state exam happens in person at a Pearson VUE test center. If you were searching for a fully online Florida real estate license: the education half is, the exam half isn't.
